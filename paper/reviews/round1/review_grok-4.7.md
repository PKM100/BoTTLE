1. SUMMARY
BoTTLE is an open roofline-plus-calibration tool that turns one latency measurement into a prediction of time, energy, and dollars per successful VLA episode on hardware the user does not own. The useful empirical claim is that eager VLA latency is mostly host/framework time, so a single multiplicative calibration factor makes Jetson transfer worse (63.3% MAPE) while splitting off that residual and scaling it by a host-CPU prior reduces error to 28.1% on 32 published pairs (Table 2). The deployment study (E3) then treats those predictions, plus published success rates, as if they identified a cost-optimal model and device; that second claim is not supported.

2. VERDICT: Major Revision — 5/10

3. STRENGTHS
- The negative result is real and is the paper's contribution. I re-ran `experiments/published.py` against the released registry: spec-only MAPE 51.8% (Jetson 54.0%), global calibration 48.8% (Jetson 63.3%), overhead-aware 28.1% (Jetson 27.7%), n=32 pairs of which 18 have Jetson targets. Table 2 matches the code. Global calibration really does multiply tens of milliseconds of non-accelerator time by the accelerator gap; that is a concrete reason not to port LLM-style single-factor predictors to eager VLA stacks.
- On the cleanest slice — the five one-reference H100-NVL→Thor pairs from arXiv 2611.06710, one per model — overhead-aware MAPE is 19% versus 67% for global calibration. The result is not only an artifact of the GR00T README groups, which are 18 of the 32 pairs.
- The authors are appropriately modest about E1. The Apple M5 numbers in Table 1 match `experiments/results/transfer.json` (overhead-aware 26.2% MAPE on 48 transfers; fp16→fp32 24.8% vs 57.3% global; SmolVLA 2-layer extrapolation APE 27%). The 104 ms vs 167 ms run-to-run note is in the saved profiles (extrapolation check 104 ms; stored full-depth measurement 167 ms). Calling E1 a smoke test rather than the decisive experiment is the right scope.
- The task-cost identities in §3.3 match `cost.py`, including the geometric 1/p correction. Mixing expected attempt length (successes end early, failures run to the horizon) with 1/p is the correct expectation, not a double count. Energy LOO numbers also match: default Thor energy MAPE 107%, leave-one-out 39%, implied power 24–79 W, u=0.6 → 86 W.
- Releasing the 31-point dataset with sources, a dependency-light CLI, and a synthetic profiler that does not need checkpoints is a legitimate artifact for a datasets/benchmarks or systems audience. The limitations section correctly says ±30% is not enough to rank configurations that are close, and that simulator success does not depend on stalls.

4. WEAKNESSES / THREATS TO VALIDITY
- **False "within 2×" claim (§4.2).** "Spec-only predictions are within 2× for all 31 cases" is not true. Eighteen of 31 predicted/measured ratios are below 0.5, i.e. worse than 2× on the low side: GR00T N1.7 on H100 is 15.6 ms vs 101.5 ms (6.5×), SmolVLA on H100 NVL is 14.9 ms vs 208.3 ms (14×). The same paragraph states the SmolVLA numbers, so the sentence contradicts itself. The released script's `within_2x` flag is APE ≤ 1, which for an under-prediction only requires pred > 0. That is not a factor-of-two bound. The geometric-mean ratio of 0.41 is the honest summary; "within 2× for all 31" must be deleted.
- **The host-prior robustness paragraph is not reproducible, and under the natural reading it is false (§4.2).** The paper says the result "is not an artifact of the host-speed priors," that σ=1 everywhere gives 31.2% MAPE (33.2% Jetson), and that halving the Jetson priors gives 35.2%. No script in `experiments/` computes this. Setting every `host_speed` to 1 in the released registry and re-running overhead-aware transfer gives 32.8% (33.4% Jetson), not 31.2/33.2. Halving edge `host_speed` (0.75→0.375, 0.45→0.225) moves Jetson MAPE from 27.7% to 64.9%, which is worse than global calibration (63.3%), not 35.2%. The claim that the finding survives halving the priors is the opposite of what the released model does. The priors are also incompletely disclosed: the text says 1.0 for server x86 and 0.45 for Orin, but `hardware.py` sets RTX 4090/5090 to 1.2, T4 to 0.8, and Orin Nano to 0.35. Those knobs affect E3's Nano and 5090 columns. I cannot tell whether 1.2 was chosen before seeing the transfer table.
- **E3's ranking claims are inside the error the authors already measured, and they ignore measurements they have (§4.3, Tables 3–4).** Overhead-aware predictions are substituted even where the dataset contains the target cell. Examples: OpenVLA Thor predicted 622 ms vs measured 445 ms (+40%); OpenVLA-OFT Thor 119 ms vs 181 ms (−34%); GR00T N1.6 Thor 152 ms vs 117 ms (+30%). Cross-source spread on the same model and device is up to 1.95× (π0.5 on Thor, 208 vs 405 ms). Against that, Table 3 boldfaces winners that differ by a few percent: +robot on the 5090 is GR00T $18.7 vs OFT $20.0 vs π0 $21.2 per 1,000 successes; +operator GR00T is $127 on the 5090 vs $129 on the 4090 vs $136 on the H100. The limitations section says ±30% cannot support fine-grained comparisons, then the tables do exactly that. Kendall τ of 0.47–0.73 (I confirm this range, on the +robot scenario, n=6 models) is not a monotone relationship; the Figure 2 caption ("cost becomes monotone in latency") overclaims. τ=0.47 on six items is four discordant pairs and is reported with no uncertainty.
- **Stack confound in the "π0 is fastest" headline (§4.3, Table 4).** π0 is anchored on the JAX π0-paper 4090 point, which the roofline already hits (71.8 vs 73 ms, ratio 0.98). Overhead-aware transfer then carries almost no residual, so π0 is predicted at 29 ms on H100. Every other model is anchored on eager PyTorch; π0.5, a sibling architecture, is predicted at 111 ms on the same H100 because its eager residual is large (roofline 26.8 ms vs measured 111.6 ms). "π0 has the lowest predicted latency on 6 of 9 devices" is largely JAX-versus-eager, not model-versus-model. The abstract's deployment moral is built on that comparison. A10G is the ninth device in the code and is required for the "6 of 9" count (π0 is fastest on 5 of the 8 devices actually shown in Table 4), but A10G appears in neither Table 3 nor Table 4.
- **Abstract device-flip does not match Table 3.** "The cost-optimal device flips from a $249 Jetson Orin Nano to an H100" is false as a general statement. In +robot+operator, synchronous, H100 is cheapest for three of six models (OpenVLA, OFT, π0). Of the four models that fit on the Nano, only π0 moves to the H100; π0.5, SmolVLA, and GR00T N1.6 move to the RTX 5090. The body states the three-of-six fact; the abstract does not. Compute-only optimality of the Nano is close to tautological: amortized Nano cost is $0.019/h versus $2.28/h for the H100 at the stated $30k / 3 years / 50% duty, while slowdown is far smaller than 100× for every model that fits. The non-obvious regime is +robot, which is exactly where the rankings are not identified.
- **Success rates and horizons are not sourced, and at least one set looks unsupported (§4.3).** The paper gives averages (OpenVLA 76.5%, OFT 97.1%, π0 94.2%, π0.5 96.9%, SmolVLA 87.3%, GR00T N1.6 97.0%) with no citation and no per-suite table. The code's OpenVLA tuple matches the OpenVLA paper; the GR00T N1.6 tuple (0.975, 0.985, 0.975, 0.945) is not traceable to the GR00T reports, which do not evaluate the standard LIBERO suites. A third-party LIBERO-Goal finetune near 97% is not a four-suite result. SmolVLA is 87.3% in the text and 87.2% in Table 3. Execution horizons "10 SmolVLA and 8 GR00T assumed" change call count by several times (`action_chunk` in the model spec is 50 for both). GR00T vs OFT on Thor in the +robot column is $22.4 vs $21.1; an assumed horizon can flip the bold cell. These rates also come from different fine-tunes, camera counts, and action chunks, then are treated as exchangeable inputs to a single ranking.
- **Transfer n=32 is not an independent sample, and the mean hides failures (§4.2, Table 2).** Twelve pairs are GR00T N1.6 from one README, each desktop device predicting every other device in the group, so errors are repeated. Effective sample size is closer to eight source groups. Overhead-aware is not uniformly better: on the fused OpenVLA-OFT group, global MAPE is 30.7% and overhead-aware is 39.5%; the single where-OFT Thor pair is 19.8% global vs 34.1% overhead-aware. Worst pair: A10G→H100 NVL, 83.6 ms predicted vs 41.8 ms measured (99.9% APE). There the residual is accelerator under-utilization, not host time, and copying it at host-speed ratio ≈1 doubles the fast-GPU prediction, while global calibration lands on 40.7 ms. The paper never states this failure mode. A host-speed-only baseline (ignore the roofline, scale the measurement by σ_r/σ_t) already reaches 35.7% Jetson MAPE versus 27.7% for the proposed split. The roofline term helps, but the paper does not show that; it only beats the straw man that multiplies host time by accelerator slowdown. "28% is close to what published data can support" because one pair differs by 1.95× is a non sequitur: max cross-source ratio is not a MAPE floor, other repeated cells are 1.01–1.40×, and the method's own max APE is 100%.
- **Energy is not solved and is not what E3 uses (§4.2).** Leave-one-out utilization is fit on the same Thor module, n=5, with implied power from 24 W to 79 W. Mean APE falls to 39%, but OpenVLA-OFT gets worse (LOO 7.3 J vs measured 14.3 J; the uncalibrated 15.6 J was closer), and SmolVLA remains 16.6 J vs 7.35 J. E3 dollars use u=0.6 anyway. No price, electricity, or duty-cycle sensitivity is reported, despite list prices the authors flag as approximate (H100 SXM at $30,000).
- **Smaller model/accounting issues, not the blocker.** The async duration max(N/f, Cℓ)+ℓ adds a full extra call when inference-bound (`cost.py`). The 0.5 GB + 10% memory slack marks OpenVLA-OFT as not fitting in a 16 GB T4 at 16.6 GB, which may be conservative. Several peaks are derived by halving sparse headlines (Thor, Orin Nano) while H100 uses tensor-core FP16 and the 4090 entry is 165 TFLOPS; the paper admits approximation, but the "JAX π0 within 2%" anecdote should not be read as validation of those peaks. Network latency for tethered datacenter GPUs is omitted, as stated.

**Single most important blocker:** E3 and the abstract present cost-optimal models and devices as findings, but those rankings are not identified. They mix a near-roofline JAX anchor for π0 with eager anchors for every other model, replace in-dataset measurements with 30–40% errors, depend on uncited and in one case untraceable success rates and assumed horizons, and boldface gaps smaller than the ±30% and 1.4–1.95× stack noise the paper itself documents. I would not accept while those claims stand.
