# Review (Round 2) — reviewer: claude-sonnet-5.5

## 1. SUMMARY
BoTTLE predicts per-call VLA latency and energy on unowned hardware from a per-stage roofline model plus a one-point "overhead-aware" calibration (the unexplained residual is carried additively), and converts it into dollars per *successful* task. Retrospective validation on 31 published latencies gives 28% MAPE; a Monte Carlo cost study on LIBERO reports only robust winners; and a new closed-loop simulation (E4) tests whether latency changes success by emulating device latency as robot holds. The contribution is a useful, honestly caveated cost-modelling tool, with an empirical story that is now much better calibrated than in Round 1, but E4's central latency-to-success finding is still confounded and is not yet connected to the E3 rankings.

## 2. VERDICT
`VERDICT: Minor Revision — 6/10`

(Borderline Major/Minor. The Round 1 issues are genuinely fixed. The remaining blockers are one E4 confound and one unfinished integration, both fixable in a revision without new framework work. I would not accept the E4 claims in the abstract and conclusion as currently worded.)

## 3. STRENGTHS
- Round 1 fixes landed and are visible in the numbers: group bootstrap CIs (19–36%), additive-residual ablation (32.8% vs 28.1%), host-prior perturbations, an eager π0 anchor with JAX as sensitivity, a measured-latency override, and robust-winner marking at P≥0.8. The paper no longer claims more than the data supports, and it states plainly that the method was designed after seeing the validation data (§E2).
- The paper reports negative and uncomfortable results. Global calibration hurts on Jetson (63% vs 54% spec-only), the method loses in the two OpenVLA-OFT groups, and the prospective SmolVLA check missed by 1.56×. Authors rarely publish that last one.
- E4 asks the right question, whether success is independent of latency, which was Round 1's biggest conceptual gap. The design is cheap, reproducible on a free T4, and the code and per-task data are released (`libero_sim_runs.json`, `bottle_libero_eval.py`). Paired per-task analysis with a task bootstrap and sign-flip test is the correct unit given the heterogeneous task difficulty.
- The paper identifies its own confound in E4. It says the 1,500 ms zero is "mostly a time-budget effect" and that holds eat the 280-step budget. I verified the arithmetic: H=10, 30 holds gives 40 steps per chunk, so 7 chunks and 70 actions. The "Max actions" column is a useful, honest addition.
- The "prediction is bounded by the software stack" framing (up to 1.95× spread for the same model and device) correctly sets the ceiling on what any predictor can achieve.

## 4. WEAKNESSES / THREATS TO VALIDITY
1. **E4's latency-to-success effect is confounded with the time budget (blocking for the E4 claims).** Holds are charged against LeRobot's 280-step budget (Table `tab:sim`, "Max actions"), so every non-stock setting both stalls the robot and shortens the episode (200/170/130/70 actions). Stock successful episodes are presumably often longer than 70 actions, and the 80→67% drop at 632 ms is likely at least partly budget truncation. The paper concedes this for 1,500 ms but still asserts a "~13 point" latency cost in the abstract, contributions, and conclusion ("stalls and lost successes"). In deployment, the time limit is arguably a policy choice, so the budget-charged result is defensible as a deployer's question, but it is **not** evidence about whether stalls destabilize control, which is exactly the assumption E3 relies on. The action-budget rerun the authors describe is the experiment that resolves this, and it is not in the paper.
2. **The headline E4 results are statistically weak, yet the abstract states them as findings.** The T4 effect is Δ=−13, CI [−28, +1], sign-flip p=0.19, so it is not significant. The Thor result is "within noise" (CI −15 to +9) at n=50 (5 per task), so the data cannot exclude a −15 point effect. The abstract's "E3's latency-independent success holds up to Thor's 308 ms" is an absence-of-evidence claim made with a CI that spans a drop large enough to change E3's rankings. An equivalence margin or a power statement is needed. Only the 1,500 ms result is significant, and it is the budget-confounded one.
3. **The stock baseline does not match the E3 inputs.** Stock E4 success is 80% on LIBERO-Spatial. E3 and Table `tab:success` use the SmolVLA paper's 90.0% for Spatial. The released `lerobot/smolvla_libero` fp32 checkpoint underperforms the published number by 10 points, and one task (task index 5 in the JSON) is at about 0% for stock, so a floor effect limits what any latency can show on that task. The paper never discusses this discrepancy. It matters because E3 uses 90% while E4 shows 80%, and `s_0/s_ℓ` ratios were computed against 80%.
4. **Heterogeneous and unequal sample sizes, and a partial row.** Settings use n=100, 100, 100, 50 and 50. The RTX 4090 row has per-task data for only 7 of 10 tasks (aggregate 77/100, with 23/30 on tasks 7–9 transcribed from a notebook summary). Its Δ is therefore computed on a different subset than its success column, which sits oddly in one table. The sign-flip test with 7–10 task-level units has very low power and a coarse p-value floor, so the "significance" language is mostly decorative. Episodes within a task also share seeds and initial states, so Wilson CIs on pooled episode counts (Table `tab:sim`) overstate precision relative to the task-level bootstrap. The two CI styles are mixed in the same table.
5. **The emulation fidelity is untested.** A hold with zero EE delta and the last gripper command is not what a real robot does under a stale action: controllers can keep moving, and a stale gripper command can release or squeeze an object. The measured "632 ms" setting is the T4's own latency, while physics is paused during the real inference, so there is no sanity check that emulated holds equal a real in-the-loop slow policy. The paper acknowledges "pure holds rather than sensor staleness," but the abstract/conclusion generalize to "slow devices pay twice."
6. **The latency used for 182 ms (RTX 4090) is an fp16-assumed prediction, while the policy runs fp32 on a T4.** The prospective check shows BoTTLE under-predicts fp32 by 36%, so the "182 ms 4090" setting is a prediction of unknown accuracy being used as an input to a validity experiment. This is circular for a claim about which devices are safe. Similarly, Thor's 308 ms is a published number for a different software stack.
7. **E4's results are not folded into E3.** The Monte Carlo rankings (Table `tab:cost`) still assume latency-independent success. The paper says this explicitly (Limitations) and reports `s_0/s_ℓ` of 1.04/1.05/1.19 as a post-hoc multiplier, but the claim "leaves E3's desktop-GPU and Thor conclusions intact" is a hand-wave: these are SmolVLA-only, Spatial-only numbers applied to the story about GR00T/OFT on all four suites. The 1.19 factor also comes from a non-significant effect. Given the E3 rankings are already close (P=0.57–0.69 between GR00T and SmolVLA on the Orins), a 1.05–1.19× SmolVLA penalty could flip them, and this is untested.
8. **E3 cost deltas still rest on predicted latencies with 28% MAPE and a worst case of 100%**, and the validation is explicitly retrospective and on a small number of effective groups (8). The prospective test in E4 (1 point, 56% off) is further evidence that the nominal CI may be optimistic. Monte Carlo resamples only the 32 pooled E2 residuals, which are correlated by group (not resampled by group here), so the error distribution is itself under-dispersed. Only one overhead mechanism is retested; the host-overhead interpretation remains untested (correctly stated).
9. **Success-rate comparability.** Table `tab:success` mixes success numbers from five codebases, with 200–500 trials and different execution horizons. E3 treats them as one comparable column. The paper notes this, but the cross-model "fastest ≠ cheapest" ranking relies on small success differences (OFT 97.1 vs GR00T 97.0 vs π0.5 96.9) that are well inside inter-codebase variation. The N_trials=200 GR00T Beta posterior is wider, but the systematic offset is not modelled.
10. **Smaller points.** (a) Abstract claims "(a) a software-stack choice that reorders latency rankings on 6 of 9 devices leaves the cheapest model unchanged," but the stack change is only the π0 anchor (JAX vs eager). That is one model on one anchor, not a general software-stack result. (b) The "Orin Nano is cheapest with P>0.99" result depends on 50% duty amortization and ignores that it is not real-time for most models; the text partly addresses this under "Embedded feasibility", but the abstract does not. (c) The \$/h robot figures and the success-fraction-of-limit values are assumptions, not data, and sampled ranges are chosen by the authors; this affects the "who pays" conclusion, which is a restatement of the cost model's own inputs. (d) E1 is `\input` and not described in the main text I could read; I could not assess it.

## 5. ARE THE NEW-THIS-REVISION CHANGES ADEQUATELY APPLIED?
- **E4 section/Table/Figure — partially.** The design is sound and honestly described, and the confound is acknowledged. But the abstract (last paragraph), contribution 5, and conclusion still present "about 13 points at ~630 ms" and "fails on slow devices" as findings, while the body says the effect is non-significant and budget-confounded. The abstract is **stronger than the evidence**. The 1,500 ms zero is rightly attributed to budget exhaustion in the body, but the abstract's "where stalls exhaust the episode's time budget" is appropriately worded; the conclusion's "fails on slow devices, which pay twice: once in stalls and again in lost successes" is not supported by a significant result that isolates stalls.
- **`s_0/s_ℓ` reported but not folded into E3 — inadequate as stated.** Honest (it is disclosed twice), but "leaves E3's conclusions intact" is asserted rather than shown. It is cheap to put the correction into the Monte Carlo as an additional sampled multiplier (with the E4 CI) and report whether any robust winner changes. This is a writing-plus-rerun-of-existing-script item, not a new experiment.
- **Prospective latency check — adequately and commendably applied**, with one caveat: it is a single point (SmolVLA, T4, fp32 vs. fp16 assumption) and the paper correctly calls it "unplanned." It should not be described as validation of the 28% figure; it argues the real out-of-sample error is larger. The text already says "outside E2's typical 28%." I would add it to the Limitations as a reason to treat the CI as optimistic. The "Colab two-vCPU host" explanation is speculative and untested; label it as such.
- **Abstract/contributions/Limitations/Conclusion updates — mostly applied; Limitations are good, Abstract and Conclusion overreach** (see above). The E1 `\input` I could not evaluate.
- **In-progress action-budget rerun — not in the paper.** This is the single experiment that decides whether E4 says anything about control stability. Until it is in, E4 supports only "with a fixed time budget, ≥1.5 s per chunk fails; ≤0.3 s is fine; 0.6 s is unclear."
- **Remaining confound vs presentational:** one confound remains (budget charging, weakness 1), which is empirical. Weaknesses 2, 3, 4, 7, and 10 are presentational or re-analysis; weaknesses 5 and 6 are empirical but can be scoped as limitations.

## 6. REQUIRED FIXES FOR ACCEPTANCE
1. **NEW-EXPERIMENT (already in progress):** Complete and report the action-budget rerun at 630 ms and 1,500 ms (holds do not consume the 280-action budget; keep the 1,000-physics-step cap), at least 10 episodes per task and all 10 tasks, using the same seeds as stock. Report both budget-charged and budget-free results side by side. Ideally add one intermediate setting (e.g. 308 ms budget-free) so that the Thor claim has a matched test.
2. **NEW-EXPERIMENT (small):** Increase n for Thor (and the 630 ms setting if the rerun is cheap) to 10 per task so all non-stress settings share n=100 and the "no effect up to 308 ms" claim has an interval narrower than ±10 points. Also finish the per-task logs for the RTX 4090 tasks 7–9 (or rerun them) so the table is internally consistent.
3. **writing-only:** Rewrite the abstract, contribution 5, and conclusion to match the evidence: "no detectable change up to 308 ms (CI −15 to +9); a non-significant ~13-point drop at 630 ms; zero at 1,500 ms, dominated by budget exhaustion." Remove "fails on slow devices, which pay twice" unless fix 1 supports it. Use an equivalence margin or a power statement for the "holds up" claim.
4. **writing-only (+ re-run of existing script):** Fold `s_0/s_ℓ` into the E3 Monte Carlo as a sampled multiplier (using the E4 task-bootstrap distribution) for the models where it is applicable, and report whether any bold/underlined entry in Table `tab:cost` changes. If it cannot be applied to non-SmolVLA models, say so and scope the "conclusions intact" sentence to SmolVLA only.
5. **writing-only:** Explain the 80% (E4 stock) vs 90% (E3, published) SmolVLA Spatial gap, including the near-zero task, and state which number E3 should use for SmolVLA. Prefer a sensitivity run of E3 with 80%.
6. **writing-only:** Use one CI procedure (task-level bootstrap, since episodes within a task share initial states) for the success column too, or label clearly that Wilson intervals treat episodes as independent. Flag that the sign-flip test with ≤10 units cannot return p below ≈0.002 and has little power.
7. **writing-only:** Scope the emulation: say explicitly that holds are a lower-fidelity model of staleness, the 182 ms and 308 ms inputs come from different stacks/precision from the policy being run, and the prospective check shows the 182 ms prediction is itself uncertain (~±36%+). Consider adding 150–250 ms and 400–500 ms points to make the curve less dependent on any single predicted latency.
8. **writing-only:** Resample the Monte Carlo latency multiplier by source group (or note that it is a pooled correlated sample), and say the prospective miss (−36%) is not included in the error distribution; optionally add it as a stress case.
9. **writing-only:** Restrict the abstract's "software stack reorders latency rankings on 6 of 9 devices" to what was tested (JAX vs eager π0 anchor), and state in the abstract that the Orin Nano "cheapest" result is mostly non-real-time.

## 7. QUESTIONS FOR THE AUTHORS
1. In the stock runs, what is the distribution of successful-episode lengths? What fraction of successes need more than 70, 130, 170, or 200 actions? This would let readers predict the budget-charged curve without any control effect.
2. Why does the released `smolvla_libero` checkpoint reach 80% on Spatial vs. the 90% in the SmolVLA paper? Is it fp32 vs. bf16, a seed or revision difference, or the task-index-5 failure? Does the same gap affect the Object/Goal/Long suites used in E3?
3. Does the hold apply the OSC controller's zero delta each step (so gravity or contacts can still move the arm) or freeze joint targets? Does the gripper hold ever cause drops, and have you looked at failure videos at 632 ms for tasks 4 and 9?
4. With the budget-free rerun, do you expect 630 ms to recover to ≈80%? If success stays ≈67%, what is the mechanism, and does it hold for H≠10?
5. Would E3's SmolVLA entries change under the 80% success and the T4-measured 624 ms (not 281 ms) latency? The prospective check suggests E3's fp16 assumption may be wrong for how the checkpoint is actually shipped. Are other E3 anchors also run in a different precision than assumed?
6. For the Monte Carlo, why sample latency residuals independently per draw rather than per source group? How sensitive are the P≥0.8 winners to that choice?
7. Is E4 meant to support only SmolVLA, or do you expect the 300 ms threshold to transfer to chunk sizes like H=5 (π0) or H=8 (OFT/GR00T), where the ratio of hold time to executed-action time differs?
