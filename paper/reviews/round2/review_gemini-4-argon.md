## 1. SUMMARY
This paper introduces BoTTLE, an analytical framework and CLI that predicts per-call inference latency, energy, and end-to-end cost per successful task for vision-language-action (VLA) models on unowned target hardware from a single reference measurement. By decomposing reference latency into a roofline-scaled accelerator component and an additive host/framework residual ("overhead-aware calibration"), BoTTLE reduces cross-device prediction error across 32 published transfer pairs from 48.8% (single-factor scaling) to 28.1% MAPE, and propagates prediction and parameter uncertainty through a 2,000-draw Monte Carlo study on LIBERO. In this revision, the authors add a closed-loop LIBERO-Spatial simulation study (E4) that emulates target-device inference latency as robot holds on a Colab T4, alongside a prospective out-of-sample check of BoTTLE's T4 latency prediction.

## 2. VERDICT
VERDICT: Minor Revision — 7/10

**Single most important remaining blocker:** In E4 (\S4.4, Table 4), charging inference hold steps against LeRobot's 280-step episode limit (`--bottle.budget=steps`) truncates the maximum allowed policy actions from 280 to 130 (at 632 ms) and 70 (at 1,500 ms), confounding closed-loop control degradation with premature episode truncation and double-counting latency when multiplying E3's synchronous cost ($D_{\text{sync}} = N/f + C\ell$, which already assumes all $N$ actions are executed) by $s_0/s_\ell$; completing and reporting the in-progress action-budget reruns (`--bottle.budget=actions` at 630 ms and 1,500 ms) is required to resolve this confound.

## 3. STRENGTHS
- **Practical problem formulation and clean dominant-effect finding:** Framing VLA evaluation around expected time, energy, and dollars per *successful* task across hardware regimes (`compute only`, `+robot`, `+robot+operator`) addresses a genuine deployment need. In E2 (\S4.2, Table 1), the paper cleanly demonstrates that eager-mode VLA inference is dominated by non-scaling overheads (geometric mean $2.4\times$ the roofline) and that carrying the residual additively cuts cross-device MAPE from 48.8% (63.3% on Jetson) to 28.1% (27.7% on Jetson), with 4.7 percentage points coming from the host-CPU prior and the bulk from the additive form itself.
- **Rigorous uncertainty quantification in E2 and E3:** Group-level bootstrap CIs over the 8 source groups (\S4.2, Appendix Table 5) properly account for within-group correlation, and E3's 2,000-draw Monte Carlo (\S4.3, Table 2) prevents over-interpreting point-estimate differences that lie inside the predictor's 28% error floor.
- **Valuable closed-loop simulation methodology (E4):** Emulating target-device chunk latency via synchronous zero-delta holds inside LIBERO on a commodity Colab T4 (\S4.4, `experiments/sim/bottle_libero_eval.py`) is a practical, zero-hardware way to test when simulator-paused success rates break down. It convincingly shows that desktop-GPU (182 ms) and Jetson Thor (308 ms) latencies do not meaningfully degrade SmolVLA success on LIBERO-Spatial.
- **Exemplary scientific honesty on the prospective T4 check:** Reporting that the out-of-sample T4 run measured 624 ms median against BoTTLE's 281 ms (fp16) and 399 ms (fp32) predictions ($1.56\times$ underprediction in fp32, $-36\%$) without retroactively tuning the T4 host prior ($\sigma = 0.8$) builds high credibility and highlights a real deployment pitfall (checkpoint default precision vs. assumed precision).

## 4. WEAKNESSES / THREATS TO VALIDITY
- **Confound between control failure and step-budget truncation in E4, and double-counting in the E3 correction (\S3.3 Eq. 4 vs. \S4.4, Table 4, Conclusion):**
  - In \S3.3 (Eq. 4), synchronous attempt duration is modeled as $D_{\text{sync}} = N/f + C\ell$, where $N$ is the number of *policy control steps* executed and $C\ell$ is added wall-clock stall time. That model assumes the robot is allowed to execute $N$ policy actions and pays for inference latency in extra wall-clock duration $C\ell$.
  - In E4 (\S4.4, Table 4), however, `--bottle.budget=steps` charges the $\lceil \ell f \rceil$ hold steps against LeRobot's 280-step episode limit (`_max_episode_steps = 280`). At 632 ms (13 hold steps per 10-action chunk) the policy is cut off after at most 130 actions; at 1,500 ms (30 hold steps per chunk) it is cut off after at most 70 actions. Because typical successful stock episodes require $>70$ (and often $>130$) actions, a perfectly stable policy with zero physical drift during holds would still fail when truncated at 70 or 130 actions.
  - Consequently, multiplying E3's synchronous cost by $s_0/s_\ell$ (\S4.4: *"Folding the measured success loss into E3 multiplies its synchronous robot cost per success by $s_0/s_\ell$: 1.04 for the RTX 4090, 1.05 for Thor, and 1.19 at the T4's latency"*) is **internally inconsistent (double-counting)**: E3's numerator ($D_{\text{sync}} = N/f + C\ell$) charges for the full un-truncated action horizon $N$ plus all $C\ell$ hold time, while E4's denominator ($s_\ell$ under `budget=steps`) penalizes success because the episode was truncated before $N$ actions could be taken. Likewise, the Conclusion's claim that slow devices *"pay twice: once in stalls and again in lost successes"* is only valid under E3's model if success still drops when holds do *not* truncate the 280-action budget (`--bottle.budget=actions`).
- **Missing per-task logs for RTX 4090 (tasks 7–9) create an apparent contradiction in Table 4 (\S4.4, `experiments/data/libero_sim_runs.json`):**
  - In Table 4 (`tab:sim`), `RTX 4090 (predicted)` has an overall success rate of `77 [68, 84]` ($n=100$) vs. `Stock`'s `80 [71, 87]` ($n=100$), yet the paired difference column reports `+0 [-7, +6] (7 tasks)` and the text states *"At the RTX 4090's predicted 182 ms it is unchanged (77% vs. 80%; paired $\Delta = 0$ on the 7 tasks with per-task logs)."*
  - Inspecting `experiments/data/libero_sim_runs.json` shows why: on tasks 0–6, both `stock` and `pred-rtx-4090` achieved exactly $54/70$ ($\Delta = 0$). All 3 net lost successes occurred in tasks 7–9 (`stock` achieved $26/30 = 86.7\%$, whereas `pred-rtx-4090` achieved $77 - 54 = 23/30 = 76.7\%$, so $\Delta = -10\%$ on tasks 7–9 and $\Delta = -3\%$ across all 10 tasks), which were excluded from $\Delta$ only because their per-task values were not transcribed from the notebook logs. Reporting $\Delta = +0$ alongside $77\%$ vs. $80\%$ is jarring and avoidable.
- **Unreproducible time-per-success numbers in \S4.4 (`8.8 s`, `11.4 s`, `13.1 s`, `18.4 s`) and minor latency median discrepancy (`632 ms` vs. `624 ms` vs. `~630 ms`):**
  - \S4.4 reports: *"Combining success with time, the robot's time per success grows from 8.8 s (stock) to 11.4 s (RTX 4090), 13.1 s (Thor) and 18.4 s (T4)."* Neither `experiments/data/libero_sim_runs.json` nor `experiments/sim_analysis.py` contains episode step counts or computes these four numbers (they come from `sim_s / k` in the Colab notebook's in-memory summary table).
  - Table 4 reports `632 ms` for `T4, measured per call` (the median of the 10 task medians in the `t4-measured` setting), \S4.4 prose refers to `~630 ms`, and the prospective latency check paragraph reports `624 ms` (the median across all 47 task medians across all 5 settings). This distinction should be stated explicitly so readers do not view 632 ms vs. 624 ms as a typo.
- **Asymmetric sample sizes and partial seed pairing in E4 (\S4.4, `experiments/sim_analysis.py`):**
  - `stock`, `t4-measured`, and `pred-rtx-4090` use $n=10$ episodes per task, whereas `pred-jetson-agx-thor` and `sweep-1500ms` use $n=5$ episodes per task. In `sim_analysis.py`, `paired()` subtracts each task's 5-episode mean (seeds 0–4) for Thor from `stock`'s 10-episode mean (seeds 0–9), rather than pairing on the shared seeds 0–4.
  - Furthermore, at $\sim 630$\,ms, $\Delta = -13$ points has a 95% CI of $[-28, +1]$ and exact sign-flip $p = 0.19$ (acknowledged as *"not significant"* in \S5), but the Abstract and Conclusion state the 13-point drop without noting its wide CI or $p = 0.19$.
- **Unexplained gap between SmolVLA's published LIBERO-Spatial success (90.0% in Table 6) and E4 stock success (80.0% in Table 4):**
  - In E3 (Table 6), SmolVLA's LIBERO-Spatial success rate is 90.0%. In E4 (Table 4), stock success is 80.0%, including 0/10 on Task 5 (which rises to 2/10 on T4-measured and 2/5 on Thor). The paper does not mention that `lerobot/smolvla_libero` is a multi-suite checkpoint trained with `n_action_steps=50` that is evaluated here with an overridden horizon $H=10$, explaining the 80% vs. 90% baseline shift.

## 5. ARE THE NEW-THIS-REVISION CHANGES ADEQUATELY APPLIED?
- **New Section E4 (Closed-loop LIBERO-Spatial simulation, Table 4, Figure 3):** *Mostly well-applied, with one blocking empirical confound in progress.* The experimental design (wrapping `lerobot-eval` to inject $\lceil \ell f \rceil$ zero-delta hold steps per action chunk) is clean and directly tests the latency-independence assumption at 0, 182, 308, $\sim$630, and 1,500 ms. The authors are transparent in \S4.4 that charging holds against the 280-step budget shrinks `Max actions` to 70 at 1,500 ms and 130 at 632 ms, and cannot separate timeout from control failure. However, until the in-progress `--bottle.budget=actions` reruns (at 630 ms and 1,500 ms) are included, the paper's E3 $s_0/s_\ell$ cost multiplier and Conclusion claim ("pay twice") remain confounded by step-budget truncation.
- **New prospective T4 latency check (\S4.4):** *Adequately and honestly applied.* The authors clearly separate precision mismatch (281 ms fp16 vs. 399 ms fp32) from the remaining cross-device transfer error (624 ms measured vs. 399 ms fp32 predicted, $1.56\times$), and explain the plausible causes (Colab's 2-vCPU virtualized host vs. the $\sigma = 0.8$ T4 prior) without cherry-picking or refitting.
- **Updates to Abstract, Contributions, Limitations, and Conclusion:** *Adequately applied, subject to softening the 630 ms / 1,500 ms causal phrasing once the action-budget rerun finishes.* The updates accurately reflect E4's numbers, though the Conclusion's statement that slow devices *"pay twice: once in stalls and again in lost successes"* should be conditioned on the action-budget vs. step-budget distinction.
